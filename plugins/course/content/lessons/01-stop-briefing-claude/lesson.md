# Lesson 1 — Stop briefing Claude

*In 4 steps.*

## What you'll get

After this lesson, you ask Claude for a piece of your work in one line, and it comes back built on what your business is: what you sell, your prices, how you work. The calls that are yours, it brings back to you instead of guessing. You'll have your brain's first pages, `company.md` and `offer.md`, and your calls in `CLAUDE.md`, all in your business's folder: open them in any text editor, or ask me to show one.

1. Give me your business, typed or from a document, and see my first drafts.
2. Bring in past proposals if you have them, and correct the drafts.
3. Tell me which calls are yours.
4. Ask for a piece of your work in a fresh session.

## Before we start

Your company brain is this folder, the one you named after your business: plain notes on your computer that I read before I work. Nothing in it is about your business yet; `CLAUDE.md` holds the rules I follow. Have ready what you'd tell someone about your business, any documents that say it, and one piece of work you'll want done.

## Step 1 — Give me your business

Think of someone minding your stall for a week. They take your price list, fix what's out of date before opening, and ask you about what isn't on it. I'm in the same spot: your price list may be a year old, your website may promise what you've stopped offering, and I can't tell. I draft only what your material says, and mark every gap `[ASK: …]`. The gaps show you exactly what only you know. So stop briefing the AI every time; start giving it your business once, where every session reads it.

What comes in is kept as it came, in `inbox/` in this folder, so keep passwords out, and what changes on its own, like this week's bookings. Client documents come in the next step.

> **▶ Your turn:** Give me your business, with nothing that names a client yet: type what you sell, to whom, at what price and how you work; or tell me in plain words where a document is that says it (your price list in Downloads, say).

## Step 2 — Bring in your proposals, and correct me

What you charged one client isn't necessarily your price, and an old proposal can contradict your price list. If I picked one, I'd be guessing, and my guess would read like fact. So where sources disagree, or a figure comes from one client's job, I mark it `[ASK]` and ask you.

I copy a proposal with a client's email or phone into `private/`, not `inbox/`: I read it, like everything you give me, and never quote it in a page. It's still in the folder. Leave out anything under an NDA. Then I ask my questions, and Claude Code shows you each page before it's written: choose Yes, or No and tell me what's true.

> **▶ Your turn:** If you have past proposals or other documents that name clients, tell me where they are; if not, say so, and I'll go straight to my questions.

## Step 3 — Tell me which calls are yours

With nothing to go on, I still answer, and my guess reads like fact. Your pages now settle the facts. The calls that are yours should come back to you, as the stall-minder would leave you whether to open on a Sunday. I need to know which calls those are.

I'll write your answer into `CLAUDE.md`, the file I read at the start of every session, and Claude Code will ask you before it's written. Then a separate reviewer reads your pages and your calls; if it says "Not yet", I'll tell you what to change.

> **▶ Your turn:** Tell me, in your own words, which kinds of decision you want brought back to you instead of me making them. A piano teacher might keep any refund for a missed lesson.

## Step 4 — Ask for a piece of your work

Only a session that starts after your brain was written shows what it does on its own. This conversation watched you build it, so it knows too much to be a fair test.

Your brain is saved in this folder, and the lesson waits for you. Restart rather than `/clear`, so the new session reads your brain from its first moment, and start `claude` in this same window, in your business's folder. Where the work reaches a call of yours, it marks it for you rather than deciding. The result is saved in `work/other/`.

> **▶ Your turn:** Type `/exit`, then `claude`, and as your first message ask for a piece of your work in one line, one that touches a call you named. Then type `/course:lesson`.
> ```
> /exit
> claude
> ```

Nothing here is graded: you're the reviewer now. Ask me to show you the result in `work/other/`, and look for the facts it took from your pages, the pages it names, the call it marked for you, and anything it states that your brain doesn't have.

> **▶ Your turn:** Tell me what the result took from your pages, how it handled your call, and anything it got wrong.

## Reference: how it's checked

### Reviews

When your brain's first pages and your calls are written, at the end of Step 3, a separate reviewer that wasn't part of our conversation reads them. If it says "Not yet", you change what it names, and I have it checked again. Once it passes, you run the test in Step 4, which is yours to see: nothing in it is graded. Then the lesson is done.

### How it's checked

The reviewer reads only two things in your brain: the summaries at the top of `company.md` and `offer.md` (the dated history below them isn't judged), and the calls section of `CLAUDE.md`. It doesn't open your documents or judge your business, which documents you brought, or the run. It judges whether your brain holds what this lesson asks of you, specifically enough to work.

**Must-haves** (all needed to pass):

1. Your pages say what you sell, to whom, at what price (or how you price, even "not set yet" with what you're aiming at) and how the work is done, with no gap left open and nothing that changes on its own, like bookings. (Step 2)
2. Your `CLAUDE.md` names the kinds of call I bring back to you instead of making, specific enough to tell whether a new call is one. (Step 3)

**Nice-to-haves** (not needed to pass, but you'll get feedback on them):

- Each call you keep says what you want brought with it (the options, what it would cost, who's waiting).
- Your brain says where the business is going: a goal, and by when.
