# Lesson 2 — Keep your brain current

*In 4 steps.*

## What you'll get

After this lesson, when something in your business changes or a client gets in touch, you drop it in and I file it everywhere it belongs, citing where it came from. I bring back to you anything that would change what your business says it is, and show you both versions when the new disagrees with the old. You'll have your brain updated in your business's folder: your first client's page in `customers/`, `log.md` recording every change, and `/check-brain` to find what's slipped.

1. Tell me one real change, and approve what it changes.
2. Bring in a client's material, and settle what disagrees.
3. Check your brain for what's slipped, and choose which pages need your yes.
4. Ask for work that depends on what's new, in a fresh session.

## Before we start

Have ready the change you noted at the end of Lesson 1, something recent or coming that replaces what your brain says now, and one client's material: a past proposal (one from Lesson 1 is fine), an enquiry, or notes on the work. With no clients yet, a prospect's enquiry or notes on the kind of client you're after. Both come in with `/bring-in`, a command your brain came with.

## Step 1 — Bring in your change

A brain goes out of date one unrecorded change at a time: the rate you raised in spring, the package you quietly dropped. Bring each change in as it happens, with its date, and every later piece of work gets it right. So stop updating your files by hand, or leaving them out of date; start telling the brain once.

Pages that say what your business is, like `offer.md`, wait for your yes: Claude Code shows the change in a box and asks before writing it. The old version stays below, dated. Keep passwords out, as before.

> **▶ Your turn:** Type `/bring-in` followed by what changed and from when, in your own words.

## Step 2 — Bring in a client, and settle what disagrees

A proposal from two years ago, an enquiry with its own terms, your notes after a job: material written at different times, for different clients, disagrees with what your business says now. Only you know which version is true, and whether one client's arrangement is your rule or an exception. So wherever they disagree I show you both, with where each came from, and never choose.

A document with the client's email or phone stays in `private/`, read and never quoted. The client gets a page in `customers/`. Leave out anything under an NDA.

> **▶ Your turn:** Type `/bring-in` and give me one client's material: tell me where a document is, or write a note in your own words.

## Step 3 — Check your brain

Pages drift as they're filed one change at a time: a summary falls behind its history, a claim loses its source. Only checking the whole brain finds what no single change shows. I take you through each finding in turn, then ask you two more questions about your pages.

When that's done, a separate reviewer reads your brain; if it says "Not yet", I'll tell you what to change.

> **▶ Your turn:** Type `/check-brain`.

Each finding and what you decided is in `log.md`; your answer on which pages need your yes is in `CLAUDE.md`, under "Pages I approve".

## Step 4 — Ask for work that uses what's new

Only a session that starts after the change shows whether your brain, not this conversation, now carries it.

The lesson waits for you. Restart rather than `/clear`, in this same window, and add no background: the point is to see what the brain carries. The result is saved in `work/other/`.

## You can now

When something changes, or a client gets in touch, you type `/bring-in`: I file it in every page it touches, keep the old version, dated, and Claude Code asks you before I rewrite what your business is. `/check-brain` finds what's slipped. In your business's folder: your client's page in `customers/`, `log.md`, any decision in `decisions/`, and your choice under "Pages I approve".

## Reference: how it's checked

### Reviews

When your change, your client's material and your check of the brain are all in, at the end of Step 3, a separate reviewer that wasn't part of our conversation reads your brain. If it says "Not yet", you change what it names, and I have it checked again. Once it passes, you run the test in Step 4, which is yours to see: nothing in it is graded.

### How it's checked

The reviewer reads your brain's pages, `log.md`, `decisions/` and `CLAUDE.md`. Which pages you mark verified, and what stays in `private/`, are yours, and aren't judged. It doesn't open your documents, judge your business, or decide whether the change was a good idea: it judges whether the brain now holds it, the way this lesson asks.

**Must-haves** (all needed to pass):

1. The page your change touches states the new version with when it applies, or that it's in effect now; a coming change shows both versions, each dated. (Step 1)
2. If you called the change a decision, its file says what was decided and why: any reason you could weigh again. (Step 1)
3. No question is left open: the pages you brought something into have no `[ASK]`, "TBD" or "?" standing for an open question left in their summaries; "not known yet" is an answer. (Step 2)
4. Your `CLAUDE.md` says which further pages need your yes, in a way anyone could recognise (a page or a kind of page), or that none do. (Step 3)
5. Every finding of your latest `/check-brain` has an outcome in `log.md`: what you decided, or that it stays as it is and why. (Step 3)

**Nice-to-haves** (not needed to pass, but you'll get feedback on them):

- Your client's page says what you'd want to remember before working with them again.
- Something you'd rather not see quoted, beyond contact details, is in `private/`, and named in `log.md`.
