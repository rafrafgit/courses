# Final task — Turn something new into finished work

*In 4 steps, on your own.*

## What you'll get

This time you do it on your own, as you will from now on. Something new comes into your business, and you take it all the way: into your brain, the brain checked, your command run on work that needs it, and what the result shows you added where it lasts, so the next run is better. You'll have it all in your business's folder: the pages it changed, the result in `work/`, and your new rule or check. When it passes, the course is complete.

1. Bring in what's new.
2. Check your brain.
3. Run your command on work that depends on it, in a fresh session.
4. Review the result, and add what it teaches you.

## Before we start

Have ready the new thing and the third piece of work you set aside at the end of Lesson 3; the work should need the new thing. Keep out what you kept out before. This time I don't name your choices or teach: you've made each before, and if you're unsure how, I'll say which lesson. What passes is on the course's reference page: type `/course:lesson read` to open it.

## Step 1 — Bring in what's new

New material is worth only what it changes in your brain. Until it's filed and settled, the next run works from the old version.

> **▶ Your turn:** Type `/bring-in` and give me what's new.

## Step 2 — Check your brain

What you just brought in may now contradict your offer, or a page from Lesson 2. Check before the work relies on it, not after it goes wrong.

> **▶ Your turn:** Type `/check-brain`.

## Step 3 — Run your command

Only a session that didn't hear today's conversation shows whether your brain, not you, carries what's new.

Your place in the course is kept. Restart rather than `/clear`, in this same window, and give your command only what this piece is about. The result is saved in `work/`, in a folder with your command's name.

> **▶ Your turn:** Type `/exit`, then `claude`, then, as your first message, your command with the piece of work. When the result is in, type `/course:lesson`.
> ```
> /exit
> claude
> ```

Your result is in `work/`, under your command's name.

> **▶ Your turn:** Ask me to show it to you.

## Step 4 — Review it, and add what it teaches

A review that changes nothing is forgotten by the next run. What you learn from a result is worth only what you write where the next run reads it. If something's wrong, that's a rule or a fact that fixes it; if nothing is, it's one more thing a good result must get right, which you'd now check for. I file it where it lasts, as in Lesson 3. Then a separate reviewer reads your brain, your command and the result.

> **▶ Your turn:** Read the result as you would before using it, and tell me the rule, fact or check it leads to.

## Reference: how it's checked

### Reviews

When your new rule or check is written, at the end of Step 4, a separate reviewer that wasn't part of our conversation reads your brain, your command and the result. If it says "Not yet", you change what it names, and I have it checked again. When it passes, the course is complete.

### How it's checked

The reviewer reads the pages your new material touched, `log.md`, your command, the result, and the file your rule or check went into. It never judges the result's quality or what you say about it: it judges whether your brain took in what's new, and whether what you added from the run concerns that result and can be checked.

**Must-haves** (all needed to pass):

1. The pages your new material touched say what's new, with when it applies where it replaces something, and leave no question open. If it changes no page's summary, the pages it added to just need no open question. (Step 1)
2. Every finding of your `/check-brain` has an outcome: what you decided, or why it stays. (Step 2)
3. From this run, you added a rule or fact that fixes something in the result, or a check a good result must also pass that applies to it, that someone could check a result against. (Step 4)

**Nice-to-haves** (not needed to pass, but you'll get feedback on them):

- Your rule says what to do instead, not only what to avoid.
- More than one thing added from the run, each filed where it lasts.
