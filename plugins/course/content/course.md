# Build a company brain for your business

*Set up the folder where your business's knowledge lives, so Claude Code knows your business without you explaining it.*

**For:** a solo founder or freelancer who runs a small business, or has just started one; not technical; has used ChatGPT or Claude in the browser; has a paid Claude plan, or is ready to get one; takes the course in Claude Code in the terminal, which the course helps them install.

**Length:** three lessons and a final task. `input.md` allows at most 3 lessons; the final task doesn't count towards them (P9). Its "about 2 hours" is Martin's intent, which nothing checks until lessons are timed (#83); no lesson shows a time.

**Outcome:** Today, a solo owner briefs the AI on their business, or on the same process, in every chat (W1). What they've built inside a chat product "vanish[es] into a black hole" and is updated by hand, if at all (W2). Even with their material in front of it, the AI ignores it: "The assistant acknowledged the document but largely ignored it… it generated a generic proposal from scratch" (W4, engagepy). Mistakes they've fixed come back (W3: SisLeah, outsider, "I correct it. It apologizes… we do it all over again").

After this course they can **run their business's knowledge through Claude Code, the way the builders of company brains do** (`patterns/company-brain.md`):
- **they hand it what they already have:** past proposals, an offer, notes, or their own words. The agent drafts the brain from it and marks every gap for them to fill;
- **the agent keeps the brain:** a folder of pages, each citing where it came from, a page for each client they bring in, and a log of every change;
- **when something changes, or a client comes in, they drop it in.** The agent files it everywhere it belongs, brings back to them whatever would change what the business is or decides, and finds what has slipped;
- **work they do again and again is one command,** done from the brain and saved in the folder, and **each correction goes where it lasts.**

They decide what's true, which calls are theirs, which version wins, what's private, and what each correction says. The agent reads, drafts, files, cites, logs and saves.

**The proof:**
- the brain itself, in a folder named after their business: the business, its offer and the clients they brought in, drafted from their material and corrected by them;
- a real change and a client's material brought in, with the conflicts they settled;
- a command for work they do again, with a correction filed where it lasts;
- the final task: something new taken from arrival to finished work, through all of it, without help.

**Own material in lessons:** yes. What the learner knows about their business, typed or in documents they already have (past proposals included, clients' names and all); one real change; a client's material; one piece of work they do again. None of it needs a sign-in.

**Test fixture:** no.

## Welcome

Welcome. In this course you turn a folder on your computer into your company brain: the place Claude Code learns your business from, so you stop explaining it every time. It's three lessons and a final task, here in the terminal, with me as your mentor, all on your own business. Take them in as many sittings as you like.

## What you'll get

By the end, you ask Claude Code for a piece of your work in one line, and it's done from what your business is: what you sell, your prices, the clients you've brought in, how you work. The calls that are yours, it brings back to you. When something changes, you drop it in and it updates the brain. Work you do again is one command, and your corrections stick. It's all in the folder named after your business: pages you can open in any text editor, a log, your command under `skills`, and its results in `work/`.

## You can now

You can now hand Claude Code your work without explaining your business, keep its knowledge current by dropping in what changes, and correct it so the fix lasts. You did it in Stop briefing Claude, Keep your brain current and Hand over a recurring job, and then on your own in the final task, Turn something new into finished work. It's all in your business's folder: the pages, `log.md`, your command under `skills`, and what it made in `work/`.

## After the course

This week, let the brain do the work as it comes. Run your command whenever that work comes up. Bring in your other clients, one at a time, and anything that changes or a new client who gets in touch, before you ask for work that depends on it. At the end of the week, type `/check-brain` and decide what it finds. Good luck. Whenever you have a question about your brain, come back and type `/course:lesson`.

## Prerequisites

- **Have:**
  - a paid Claude plan that includes Claude Code: Pro, Max, Ultra, Team or Enterprise ("The free claude.ai plan does not include Claude Code access", T1);
  - their own computer, where they can install software: a Mac with macOS 13 or later, or Windows 10 1809 or later, with 4 GB of memory (T2).
- **The learner's folder** (`SPEC.md`, "The learner's folder"): this is the school's first course, so it has the learner make the folder their business's knowledge will live in, named after the business by default (`~/rosa-bakery`, say). Every later course runs in the same folder. The course's own files go in `course-material/` inside it, apart from the brain.
- **The data the course needs (P13):** `input.md`, word for word: "what they know about their business, told in their own words: what they sell, to whom, at what price, and how they work; documents they already have, dropped into the folder as files: for example an offer or price list, a website's text, past proposals, notes; clients' details, names included: past proposals as they are, who a client is and what the work was; no sign-in to any of their own tools, and no passwords, logins or anything they wouldn't put into an AI tool." **Conflicts flagged:**
  - **Documents often live in chat products or cloud tools** (M1). Lesson 1 teaches getting one onto the computer (a PDF; Notion's Markdown export; a Google Doc printed to PDF, T19), then saying where it is; the agent copies it into the brain (T17). "Dropped into the folder" becomes "told where it is" (on the Decide list).
  - **Word files can't be read directly.** The agent reads them through a text copy beside the original (M4; the pattern's A4). Tested on a Mac only.
  - **Clients' details are allowed, but some of what's in a client's material shouldn't sit in the brain:** contact details (to `private/`, the pattern's fit), and anything under an NDA or given in confidence ("anything they wouldn't put into an AI tool"). Lesson 1 teaches it where client material first comes in.
  - **Where the chats go depends on the learner's plan and setting** (T21). On a consumer plan, client details reach model training if the learner allows it. Lesson 1 has them check the setting before client material comes in. The data line doesn't mention it; it's a reading of "anything they wouldn't put into an AI tool" (on the Decide list).
  - **The job for Lesson 3 must be one the brain can do without the learner's tools:** drafting, preparing, checking or analysing, not sending, booking or paying (`input.md`: no sign-ins; J3's inbox needs a connector).
- **Know:** how to type, and copy and paste. No terminal experience.
- **Setup page before Lesson 1** (not a lesson; no skill writes it yet, see the Decide list):
  - open the terminal (Anthropic's terminal guide, T20);
  - on Windows, install Git for Windows. Claude Code calls it "recommended" and falls back to PowerShell without it (T2), but the course's own setup and hooks are shell scripts (`plugins/course/scripts/`, `hooks/`), so the course requires it. On a Mac, nothing extra: git is used if it's there, and a Mac's placeholder git without the Command Line Tools counts as none (T16; the setup script checks it);
  - install Claude Code (T2) and log in;
  - make the business's folder, named after the business, and start Claude Code in it;
  - install the course plugin. At the first `/course:lesson`, its setup puts the brain's starting files in the folder (the scaffold), turns auto memory off for the folder (T6), and starts history where git is installed.
- **Permission mode:** the course runs in the mode a session starts in: auto mode in a current Claude Code (T14). Everyday writes run without a prompt. Claude Code's file tools ask before writing the brain's tier-2 files, because of the folder's own rules (T13).

**Before you buy:** five checks.
1. Do you have a Claude Pro plan or above, or are you ready to get one?
2. Is it your own Mac with macOS 13 or later, or your own PC with Windows 10 (version 1809) or later, with at least 4 GB of memory, where you can install software? On Windows, the setup page also has you install Git for Windows; the course hasn't yet been tried on Windows.
3. Can you follow a page that asks you to open a terminal window and paste a command into it? The page walks you through the rest: logging in, making your business's folder, and adding the course.
4. Your business:
   - Can you say in a sentence or two what you sell, to whom, at what price and how you work, or do you have documents that say it (an offer, a price list, your website's text, past proposals)? Either is enough; documents save you typing.
   - One thing that has changed in your business recently, or is about to, that replaces something you'd say about it today: a price, what an offer includes, a way you work.
   - Something about one of your clients: a past proposal, an enquiry, or notes on the work. Just started, with no clients yet? A prospect's enquiry, or notes on the kind of client you're after, works too.
   - For the final task, one more new thing your work will need: another client's or prospect's material, another change, or a past piece you held back.
   - **One piece of work you do again, however often:** something you'd hand to an assistant who knows your business, and check before you use it. A proposal for each new client, a report after each project, a reply to a question clients often ask, a post, a check of a draft against your standards. Bring at least three real pieces of it: one to run, one to run again after a correction, and one for the final task. Past pieces and work coming up both count. It shouldn't need your email, calendar or other tools: the course doesn't connect to them.
5. Will client details you bring in be fine to work on in Claude? Leave out anything under an NDA, or told to you in confidence. In Lesson 1 we'll check whether your Claude plan's setting lets your chats be used for training, before any client material comes in.

If you can't yet say what you sell, this course comes too early: a company brain needs a business to describe.

## Learning path

**The pattern** (`patterns/company-brain.md`, checked 2026-11-17) is the course's method. What it fixes, the course sets up as a requirement; what it leaves to the person is the learner's (P6).
- **Set up by the course** (the scaffold, written by `write-lesson` with Lesson 1; the mentor or the scaffold writes it; never judged):
  - **the tree:** `CLAUDE.md`, `index.md`, `log.md`, `decisions/`, `customers/`, `notes/`, `inbox/`, `private/`, `archive/` and `work/`, and the `bring-in` and `check-brain` skills. `company.md`, `offer.md` and `voice.md` are in the map, and written when there's something for them;
  - `CLAUDE.md`'s map and rules, with no facts in it;
  - a page's shape: summary above, dated and sourced timeline below;
  - **a page per client** in `customers/`, by name, with contact details and anything sensitive in `private/` (the pattern's fit);
  - decisions as dated files, never edited;
  - the inbox kept as it came, with a text copy beside a file the agent can't read; a document holding a client's contact details kept as it came in `private/` instead, and filed from there;
  - the log and the index; where git is installed, a commit after every change, hidden;
  - **every run of a job saves its result to `work/<job>/YYYY-MM-DD-<topic>.md`, and a request with no command to `work/other/`** (the pattern's Flow 2; `SPEC.md`, "State");
  - the two tiers' defaults, with ask rules in the folder's settings for the tier-2 files (T13), and the rest of tier 2 in `CLAUDE.md`'s rules;
  - all of it in place at the first `/course:lesson`, skills included (T7);
  - **left out:** the pattern's `people/` (a freelancer's people are their clients, in `customers/`; partners and suppliers can come in later with `/bring-in`). **Set up but not taught:** `archive/`, since no lesson retires a page; archiving asks first when it comes.
- **Drafting from the learner's material** (`SPEC.md`, P6): where the agent drafts a page from what the learner brought, it drafts only what the material states, marks every gap `[ASK: …]`, and fills none. Where two sources disagree, or a fact comes from a one-off document (a proposal's price for one client), it marks that `[ASK: …]` too and doesn't choose. The learner's part is correcting and approving the draft and filling the gaps.
- **The learner's:**
  - what's true: correcting and approving each draft, and filling each gap;
  - which kinds of call are theirs (Lesson 1), and at a gap, ask first or mark it (Lesson 3, where a job first meets one);
  - which documents come in, whether chats may train models (their plan's setting), and what's private;
  - each tier-2 change, and which version wins in a conflict;
  - which pages beyond the defaults need their yes (the pattern: "The person can move things between tiers");
  - each decision and its reasons;
  - what `/check-brain` finds, and which pages they've checked;
  - the job, what a good result must get right, and what it brings back to them;
  - each correction's wording and reach.

**Help shrinks** (P4):
- In Lesson 1 the mentor names each decision and teaches the idea behind it, with a contrast from another situation.
- In Lesson 2 it names the decisions and teaches the idea only.
- In Lesson 3 the learner names what their job needs from what Lessons 1–2 taught, and the mentor asks only about what they missed. Two ideas are new there and taught once, plainly: what a command is (T7), and a correction's reach.
- **The final task** has no help: the mentor shows each step's ask and checks that the step happened, and teaches nothing new.

**The pattern's words stay out of the learner's text.** "Tier 2", "Flow 3", "root file" and "scaffold" are the design's shorthand. `write-lesson` says what they mean: "the files that say what your business is", "when the new disagrees with the old", "your brain's folders and files".

**Final task: Turn something new into finished work** (P9). No help: the mentor shows each ask and checks the step happened, and teaches nothing.
- **Capability:** the whole promise, on their own: Claude Code works from a brain they keep current, and their corrections last.
- **The job:** "Something new came in and I took it all the way myself: into the brain, through my command, corrected, and the brain checked." Something new arrives that the learner's job will need: a new client's or prospect's enquiry, a changed price, a new piece of past work.
- **Replaces:** updating the AI's knowledge, then briefing it, then fixing its output, each by hand, every time something new comes in (W1, W2, W3).
- **The steps:**
  1. Bring it in with `/bring-in`, deciding what's true, what's private and which version wins (Lesson 2; Lesson 1's what's-true).
  2. In a fresh session, run the command on real work that depends on it (Lesson 3, with the brain from Lesson 1: the calls that are theirs are brought back).
  3. Review the result, and correct what's wrong where the fix lasts (Lesson 3). If nothing is wrong, it's done.
  4. Run `/check-brain` and decide each finding (Lesson 2).
- **What it brings together:** Lesson 1's core and calls (the run works from them and brings the calls back), Lesson 2's bringing in and checking, and Lesson 3's command and corrections.
- **Artifact, and "You decide":** the brain and the command, changed by the learner alone: what's true in the new material, what's private, which version wins, each correction and its reach, each finding. **Required:** as in the lessons: inbox, log, index, commits, the result saved in `work/<your-job>/`.
- **Evidence in the workspace:**
  - the new material in `inbox/` (or `private/`, if it holds contact details) with its `filed` line;
  - the pages it changed, sourced, with tier-2 changes approved;
  - a result in `work/<your-job>/`, newer than the bring-in, citing the new material;
  - a correction with its `log.md` line, if the run had something wrong;
  - the `/check-brain` findings decided in the log or the pages.
- **When it passes:** the course is complete. The mentor shows "You can now" and "After the course".
